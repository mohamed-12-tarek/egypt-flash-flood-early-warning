# Before the rain

- Know whether you are in or near a wadi (dry riverbed) or at the mouth of one. Flash floods follow these channels and can arrive from rain that fell far upstream, even under a clear sky.
- Do not camp, park or build in a wadi bed or at its exit.
- Keep a basic emergency kit: drinking water, torch, power bank, first-aid supplies, important documents in a waterproof bag.
- Agree on a higher-ground meeting point with family or group members.

# When a warning or heavy rain is reported

- Move to higher ground immediately. Do not wait to see the water arrive.
- Leave the wadi and low-lying areas; get away from channels, culverts and underpasses.
- Follow instructions from the authorities and local civil protection.
- Keep a charged phone and check official sources for updates.

# If you are caught in floodwater

- Never walk, swim or drive through floodwater. Fast water can sweep away people and vehicles in moments, and depth is hard to judge.
- If a vehicle is surrounded by water, leave it and move to higher ground if you can do so safely.
- Avoid power lines and fallen cables.

# Travelling

- Do not cross flooded roads, bridges or dips in the road. If a road is flooded, turn around and use another route.
- Avoid mountain roads and wadi crossings during and after heavy rain; rock falls and washouts are common.
- Tell someone your route and expected arrival time.

# After the flood

- Stay away from floodwater, which may be contaminated or hide damaged roads.
- Do not return to an affected area until authorities say it is safe.
- Report missing persons and hazards to the authorities.
